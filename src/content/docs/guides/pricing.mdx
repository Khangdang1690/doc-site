This page compares what you'd actually pay each month to run sqlitedeploy
on rented compute + a bucket vs running the same workload on managed
PostgreSQL. Tone is honest engineering: we'll show where sqlitedeploy
saves real money, where it doesn't apply, and where managed Postgres is
worth its price.

<Aside type="note">
	**Prices fetched 2026-05-09.** Cloud pricing drifts; every figure
	below links to its source so you can re-verify before quoting these
	numbers in a decision document. US-East / us-central1 / EU regions
	used where applicable; reserved/committed-use discounts ignored
	(on-demand only).
</Aside>

## TL;DR — small production app

Baseline: **10 GB of data, ~5M queries/month, ~100 GB egress/month, single
region, business-hours bursty traffic.** Roughly the shape of an early-stage
SaaS or a moderately-active internal tool.

| ---------------------------------------------- | -------- | --------- | -------- | ----------------: |
| sqlitedeploy on Hetzner CX22 + Cloudflare R2   | €3.79    | $0.15     | $0       | **~€4 (~$5)**     |
| sqlitedeploy on Fly shared-cpu-1x 1 GB + R2    | $5.92    | $0.15     | $0       | **~$6**           |
| sqlitedeploy on AWS t4g.small (EC2) + S3       | ~$12.26  | $0.23     | $9.00    | **~$22**          |
| Supabase Pro                                   | included | included  | included | **$25** (10 GB+egress in plan) |
| RDS Postgres db.t4g.small, single-AZ           | $23.36   | ~$1.15    | ~$9.00   | **~$34**          |
| Neon Launch (24/7 active, 1 CU)                | ~$77     | $3.50     | varies   | **~$80**          |
| RDS Postgres db.t4g.small, Multi-AZ            | $46.72   | ~$2.30    | ~$9.00   | **~$58**          |

The savings aren't a quirk of the math — they come from the architecture.
sqlitedeploy is a single process and a bucket. Managed Postgres is a
managed service with a built-in compute floor.

## What "serverless" actually means for cost

<Aside type="caution">
	**"Serverless" ≠ "free when idle."** Each service draws the line
	differently, and three of the most-quoted serverless Postgres products
	have very different idle bills.
</Aside>

- **Aurora Serverless v2** scales between a *minimum* and *maximum* ACU.
  Setting the minimum to **0 ACU** lets it pause when there are no active
  connections, but
  [resume takes ~15 seconds](https://repost.aws/questions/QUbtHMLZXiS4Kppi7KMIB5YQ/aurora-serverless-v2-minimum-cost-setup-for-development-environment) —
  acceptable for dev, not for user-facing production. Setting the minimum
  to 0.5 ACU keeps it warm and bills $0.12 × 0.5 × 730 = **~$43.80/month
  even at zero traffic**, before storage.
- **Neon** scales to zero genuinely — suspended computes bill $0/hour
  ([source](https://neon.com/pricing)). Cold-start is faster than Aurora
  but still measurable. The flip side is that an *active* Neon Launch
  database at 1 CU is $0.106/CU-hr × 730 = ~$77/month before storage,
  more than RDS Multi-AZ.
- **sqlitedeploy** is *not* scale-to-zero. The primary VM runs 24/7 and
  bills the same whether traffic exists or not. The trade is that the
  always-on bill is **~$5/month**, not $40+/month — so for any workload
  that gets meaningful traffic during business hours, sqlitedeploy is
  cheaper than scale-to-zero serverless even before considering
  cold-start UX.

The honest framing: **sqlitedeploy is "always-on cheap," not
"scale-to-zero."** If your workload genuinely sees zero traffic for most
of the day and you can tolerate cold-starts, Neon's free tier or scale-to-zero
billing wins. If it sees any sustained traffic, the $5 VM beats every
managed-Postgres compute floor.

## Where the savings come from

1. **No managed-Postgres compute floor.** A €3.79 Hetzner CX22 is cheaper
   than the cheapest RDS instance — and it has 4 GB of RAM, twice what
   t4g.small ships with.
2. **Free egress on R2.** S3 charges $0.09/GB after the first 100 GB;
   100 GB/month of egress on S3 is ~$9 you don't pay on R2. For backup
   restore over the wire (which can be tens of GB), this matters.
3. **One writer = no replication billing surprises.** No Multi-AZ doubling,
   no read-replica compute hours. Replicas in sqlitedeploy run on
   *additional* commodity VMs you control — same price ceiling, not the
   2× managed-service uplift.
4. **Free Cloudflare quick tunnels for staging and hobby.** Production
   typically wants a real domain, but for everything earlier, the tunnel
   is free and the URL is real.
5. **No connection-pooler add-on.** The Hrana protocol is HTTP — Lambdas,
   Workers, and Vercel functions can hammer it without exhausting
   anything. Postgres needs PgBouncer or RDS Proxy ($) to do the same.
6. **Embedded replicas replace your Redis cache.** Each app instance's
   local SQLite serves reads at microsecond latency directly from the
   app process — there's no Redis sitting next to Postgres to make
   reads fast, no cache-eviction story to manage, no separate billing
   line. The "Postgres + Redis" reference architecture collapses into
   one writer + your existing app fleet.

## Where managed Postgres wins on cost

- **Genuinely idle workloads on Neon Free or Scale-to-zero.** sqlitedeploy
  on a $5 VM still costs $5 when nobody is using it. Neon costs $0.
  For demo apps, internal tools that hibernate over weekends, or per-tenant
  preview databases, scale-to-zero is the right tool.
- **Workloads where ops time exceeds the savings.** RDS auto-failover is
  paid-for engineering. If your team would spend 3 engineer-hours/month
  on a sqlitedeploy primary that RDS would handle automatically, RDS is
  cheaper at any reasonable hourly rate.
- **Workloads that need >1 writer or strict cross-region durability.**
  sqlitedeploy *can't do this* — comparison doesn't apply. Postgres,
  Aurora Global Database, or Spanner are the right tools.
- **Heavy use of Postgres-only features.** RLS, mature JSONB indexing,
  PostGIS, pg_vector at scale, listen/notify, advisory locks, materialized
  views — these are real and they're on Postgres. SQLite has rougher
  equivalents at best.

## Three sample workloads

### Hobby / side-project

1 GB data, ~10K queries/day, occasional traffic.

| Option                          | Monthly | Notes |
| ------------------------------- | ------: | ----- |
| sqlitedeploy on Hetzner CX22 + R2 free tier | **~€4** | well within R2's 10 GB free |
| Supabase Free                   | **$0**  | 500 MB DB ceiling — fine for 1 GB? not quite |
| Neon Free                       | **$0**  | 0.5 GB ceiling — same constraint |
| RDS db.t4g.micro single-AZ      | ~$15    | $11.68 compute + ~$1 storage + small egress |
| Aurora SLv2 with 0.5 ACU floor  | ~$45    | floor dominates the bill |

If your data fits in the free tiers, free tiers win. As soon as it
doesn't, sqlitedeploy is cheapest.

### Small SaaS

10 GB data, ~5M queries/month, ~50K MAU, real-user expectations.

| Option                                | Monthly | Notes |
| ------------------------------------- | ------: | ----- |
| sqlitedeploy on Hetzner CX22 + R2     | **~€5** | scales without changing the bill until ~50 GB |
| sqlitedeploy on Fly shared-cpu-1x 1 GB + R2 | **~$8** | nicer ops UX than raw VM |
| Supabase Pro                          | **$25** | DX-first, no VM, generous egress |
| RDS Multi-AZ db.t4g.small             | **~$58** | failover handled |
| Aurora Postgres SLv2 (~0.75 avg ACU)  | **~$80** | scales smoothly |
| Cloud SQL 1 vCPU 4 GB                 | **~$64** | similar shape to RDS |

Cost ratio sqlitedeploy : RDS Multi-AZ ≈ **1 : 10**. The honest counter
is that RDS Multi-AZ does paid-for things sqlitedeploy doesn't (auto
failover, point-in-time recovery without thought).

### Read-mostly CRUD app (replacing Postgres + Redis)

10 GB data, ~5M queries/month, heavy read-fanout — the canonical
"Postgres for storage, Redis in front for hot reads" stack.

| Option                                                | Monthly | Notes |
| ----------------------------------------------------- | ------: | ----- |
| sqlitedeploy on Hetzner CX22 + R2                     | **~€5 (~$6)** | embedded replicas in each app instance serve reads — no separate cache |
| Render Postgres Starter + Render Redis Starter        | **~$17** | $7 + $10; cheapest managed, no HA, small instances |
| Supabase Pro + Upstash Pay-as-you-go                  | **~$30** | $25 + ~$5 commands; serverless DX |
| RDS Multi-AZ db.t4g.small + ElastiCache cache.t4g.micro | **~$70** | $58 + ~$12; the "boring" AWS production stack |
| Neon Launch (1 CU 24/7) + Upstash                     | **~$82** | scale-to-zero possible but eats cold-start UX |

The architectural point is that the **Redis line disappears**, not that
Postgres is replaced by something cheaper feature-for-feature. The local
embedded SQLite plays the role Redis usually plays in this stack: hot
reads served out of the app's own filesystem, no network hop, no cache
invalidation logic. You're saving on Postgres *plus* the cache layer
people usually put in front of it, which is where the gap really opens up.

### Read-heavy edge app

10 GB data, reads served from 5 application instances across regions for
low-latency local reads.

| Option                                                | Monthly | Notes |
| ----------------------------------------------------- | ------: | ----- |
| sqlitedeploy: 1 primary (CX22) + R2, embedded replicas in your 5 app instances | **~€4 (~$5)** | replicas live inside your existing app processes — zero added compute |
| sqlitedeploy: 1 primary + 5 dedicated replica VMs (CX22 each) + R2 | **~€23 (~$25)** | fallback for stacks without libSQL client bindings; replicas scale linearly in cost and reads |
| RDS Multi-AZ + 5 cross-region read replicas           | **$200+** | each replica is its own paid instance + cross-region transfer |
| Aurora Global Database (1 primary + 5 secondaries)    | **$300+** | every secondary bills compute + storage replication |
| Neon read replicas                                    | **varies** | Neon supports compute branches, billed per CU-hour |

This is where the architecture pays out most. The default path —
**embedded replicas inside the app instances you're already running** —
adds zero compute: reads come out of each app's local SQLite at
microsecond latency, the writer is unchanged, and managed-service
per-replica uplifts disappear. The dedicated-replica-VM path is the
fallback when your runtime can't embed the libSQL client (e.g.,
languages without bindings).

<Aside type="caution">
	**What these numbers don't include:**
	- Engineer-hours running the VM. RDS Multi-AZ handles failover automatically; sqlitedeploy expects you to.
	- Cross-region data transfer in either direction (typically $0.02–$0.09/GB on AWS).
	- Backup-restore time and operational drills. With sqld + bottomless, restore is `--sync-from-storage` against your bucket.
	- The cost of the migration itself if you're moving off Postgres. SQL dialect differences, JSON handling, and migration tooling add real engineering hours.
	- Premium support contracts (relevant for RDS/Aurora/Cloud SQL at the Business tier).
</Aside>
